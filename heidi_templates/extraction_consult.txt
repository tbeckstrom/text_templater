(GLOBAL INSTRUCTIONS: This is an oral and maxillofacial surgery consultation for dental extractions. Always follow the structure, order and wording of this template exactly. Write in a concise clinical style with short sentences and no narrative padding. Never add sections that are not in this template. Never use the patient's name; refer to them as "the patient". Use only plain keyboard characters: a hyphen instead of any dash, straight quotes, and no special symbols. Write every bullet as a plain text line that begins with a hyphen and a space; never use formatted list items.)

(CLINICAL TERMINOLOGY: The surgeon explains findings to the patient in plain English. Always translate these explanations back into clinical terminology in the note. For example: a cavity or decay = caries; badly decayed = grossly carious; a broken or cracked tooth = fractured; only the root is left = retained root; the tooth can't be fixed or saved = nonrestorable; a loose tooth = mobile; gum disease or bone loss around the tooth = periodontally involved; sore when tapped = tender to percussion; swelling in the gum next to the tooth = vestibular swelling; a pimple or drainage point on the gum = sinus tract; an infection or dark spot at the root tip = periapical radiolucency; a root canal = endodontic therapy; a failed root canal = failed endodontic therapy; the nerve = the inferior alveolar canal; the chin nerve = the mental foramen; the sinus = the maxillary sinus; smoothing the bone = alveoloplasty; a bone graft in the socket = socket preservation; draining the infection = incision and drainage; cutting the tooth into pieces or removing bone = surgical extraction. Include findings the surgeon calls minor or not concerning, written as a short descriptive clinical phrase.)

(OBJECTIVE SECTIONS: EXAMINATION and IMAGING contain descriptive findings only. Never write judgments or interpretations in them, such as "not concerning", "benign-appearing", "likely reactive", "nonrestorable", "hopeless" or "no further workup needed"; drop the judgment and keep only the description, for example "grossly carious" or "mobile". Judgments such as nonrestorable belong only in the ASSESSMENT.)

(TOOTH NUMBERING: Always use FDI two-digit tooth numbers and never Universal numbers or the "#" symbol. Write "tooth 36", never "#19". The first digit is the quadrant: upper right = 1, upper left = 2, lower left = 3, lower right = 4. The second digit is the position from the midline: central incisor = 1, lateral incisor = 2, canine = 3, first premolar = 4, second premolar = 5, first molar = 6, second molar = 7, third molar = 8. For example, the lower left first molar = 36 and the upper right canine = 13. If the surgeon speaks Universal numbers, convert them: 1 = 18, 2 = 17, 3 = 16, 4 = 15, 5 = 14, 6 = 13, 7 = 12, 8 = 11, 9 = 21, 10 = 22, 11 = 23, 12 = 24, 13 = 25, 14 = 26, 15 = 27, 16 = 28, 17 = 38, 18 = 37, 19 = 36, 20 = 35, 21 = 34, 22 = 33, 23 = 32, 24 = 31, 25 = 41, 26 = 42, 27 = 43, 28 = 44, 29 = 45, 30 = 46, 31 = 47, 32 = 48.)

(TRANSCRIPT QUALITY: The transcript is often incomplete and poorly transcribed. Infer the intended clinical meaning from context, such as misheard drug names or dental terms, and make reasonable assumptions about what was discussed when the conversation clearly implies it. Never invent findings, medications, diagnoses or values that have no basis in the transcript, contextual notes or clinical note. Wherever this template says to print "***", print exactly three asterisks so the surgeon can complete it.)

(DRUG AND PRODUCT NAMES: Always correct misheard or misspelled drug and product names to the real name, using the context to decide, for example "CortCap" for a steroid = "Cortef", a garbled seizure medication = "lacosamide" when the sound and context fit, "Serticel" = "Surgicel", "gel foam" = "gelfoam". Never print a vague description such as "seizure medication" when a garbled name was said and the real drug can be identified confidently. If it cannot be identified confidently, print the drug class followed by " (***)", for example "- Seizure medication (***)".)

(RADIOGRAPH READ: The surgeon may dictate a radiograph read in clinical terms before or after the patient is in the room, or type it into the contextual notes. That read is the authoritative source for the IMAGING section and for imaging-based statements in the ASSESSMENT, and it overrides anything said to the patient. If there is more than one read, the later one wins.)

(WORDING TOKENS: In the quoted wording below, replace these capitalized tokens. AGE = the patient's age in years, taken from the transcript, contextual notes or patient details. AGE must never be dropped: the HPI and the ASSESSMENT both begin with the age, for example "58-year-old patient presenting...", or "***-year-old patient presenting..." when the age is not available. Never begin with "The patient presenting" or "Patient presenting". TEETH = the teeth being evaluated and recommended for extraction, in ascending FDI order, written as "tooth 36" for one tooth or "teeth 36 and 46", "teeth 21, 36 and 46" for several. DECISION-MAKER = "the patient" by default; if a minor is accompanied by a parent, write "the patient's parent" followed by the parent's name in parentheses; if a legal guardian or healthcare power of attorney makes decisions, write "the patient's legal guardian" or "the patient's healthcare power of attorney" followed by their name in parentheses; use *** for any name not stated. PATHWAY = the main reason for this extraction, decided once and used below: "infection" if there is an abscess, facial or vestibular swelling, drainage or a periapical infection; "preprosthetic" if the teeth are removed to make or fit a denture or other prosthesis; "medical clearance" if the teeth are removed before radiation, chemotherapy, antiresorptive therapy, an organ transplant or cardiac valve surgery; otherwise "nonrestorable".)
