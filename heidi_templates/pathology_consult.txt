(GLOBAL INSTRUCTIONS: This is an oral and maxillofacial surgery consultation for an oral soft tissue lesion or a jaw lesion. Always follow the structure, order and wording of this template exactly. Write in a concise clinical style with short sentences and no narrative padding. Never add sections that are not in this template. Never use the patient's name; refer to them as "the patient". Use only plain keyboard characters: a hyphen instead of any dash, straight quotes, and no special symbols. Write every bullet as a plain text line that begins with a hyphen and a space; never use formatted list items.)

(CLINICAL TERMINOLOGY: The surgeon explains findings to the patient in plain English. Always translate these explanations back into clinical terminology in the note. For example: spot, patch or sore = lesion; bump or lump = nodule or mass; white patch = white lesion; red patch = red lesion; open sore = ulcerated; rough or cauliflower-like = verrucous; finger-like or warty = papillary; hard = firm; very hard or stuck down = indurated; squishy or fluid-filled = fluctuant; on a stalk = pedunculated; broad base = sessile; does not rub off = non-wipeable; rubs off = wipeable; side of the tongue = lateral tongue; underside of the tongue = ventral tongue; top of the tongue = dorsal tongue; under the tongue = floor of mouth; inside of the cheek = buccal mucosa; roof of the mouth = hard palate; back of the roof of the mouth = soft palate; gums = gingiva; behind the last molar = retromolar trigone; dark area or hole in the bone on the x-ray = radiolucency; bright or white area on the x-ray = radiopacity; bone bulging = expansion; thin bone = cortical thinning; roots eaten away = root resorption; glands in the neck = cervical lymph nodes; numb lip or chin = altered sensation; tooth not alive = no response to cold testing. Include findings the surgeon calls minor, as a short descriptive clinical phrase.)

(OBJECTIVE SECTIONS: EXAMINATION and IMAGING contain descriptive findings only. Never write judgments or interpretations in them, such as "not concerning", "benign-appearing", "looks like a fibroma", "likely reactive", "suspicious" or "no further workup needed"; drop the judgment and keep only the description. Diagnoses and level of concern belong only in the ASSESSMENT.)

(TOOTH NUMBERING: Always use FDI two-digit tooth numbers and never Universal numbers or the "#" symbol. Write "tooth 36", never "#19". A run of adjacent teeth is written as a span, for example "36-38". If the surgeon speaks Universal numbers, convert them to FDI.)

(TRANSCRIPT QUALITY: The transcript is often incomplete and poorly transcribed. Infer the intended clinical meaning from context, such as misheard drug names or anatomical terms, and make reasonable assumptions about what was discussed when the conversation clearly implies it. Never invent findings, measurements, medications, diagnoses or values that have no basis in the transcript, contextual notes or clinical note. Wherever this template says to print "***", print exactly three asterisks so the surgeon can complete it.)

(RADIOGRAPH READ: The surgeon may dictate a radiograph or CBCT read in clinical terms before or after the patient is in the room, or type it into the contextual notes. That read is the authoritative source for the radiographic findings of each intraosseous lesion, for the IMAGING section and for imaging-based statements in the ASSESSMENT, and it overrides anything said to the patient. If there is more than one read, the later one wins.)
