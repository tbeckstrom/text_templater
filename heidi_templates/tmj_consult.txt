(GLOBAL INSTRUCTIONS: This is an oral and maxillofacial surgery consultation for temporomandibular joint (TMJ) pain. Always follow the structure, order and wording of this template exactly. Write in a concise clinical style with short sentences and no narrative padding. Never add sections that are not in this template. Never use the patient's name; refer to them as "the patient". Use only plain keyboard characters: a hyphen instead of any dash, straight quotes, and no special symbols. Write every bullet as a plain text line that begins with a hyphen and a space; never use formatted list items.)

(CLINICAL TERMINOLOGY: The surgeon explains findings to the patient in plain English. Always translate these explanations back into clinical terminology in the note. For example: jaw joint = TMJ; popping or clicking = click; grinding or gravel sound in the joint = crepitus; jaw gets stuck = locking; cannot open all the way = limited opening; jaw muscles = muscles of mastication; cheek muscle = masseter; temple muscle = temporalis; jaw swings to one side and comes back = deviation with return to midline; jaw goes to one side and stays there = deflection without correction; grinding teeth at night = nocturnal bruxism; clenching during the day = daytime clenching; bite feels off = a change in the bite; pressing on the joint while biting hurts = pain on dynamic loading; tongue has ridges = tongue scalloping; line on the inside of the cheek = linea alba; worn teeth = attritional wear. Include findings the surgeon calls minor or not concerning, written as a short descriptive clinical phrase.)

(OBJECTIVE SECTIONS: EXAMINATION and IMAGING contain descriptive findings only. Never write judgments or interpretations in them, such as "not concerning", "benign-appearing", "likely muscular", "normal variant" or "no further workup needed"; drop the judgment and keep only the description.)

(TOOTH NUMBERING: If any tooth is mentioned, always use FDI two-digit tooth numbers and never Universal numbers or the "#" symbol.)

(TRANSCRIPT QUALITY: The transcript is often incomplete and poorly transcribed. Infer the intended clinical meaning from context, such as misheard drug names or anatomical terms, and make reasonable assumptions about what was discussed when the conversation clearly implies it. Never invent findings, measurements, medications, diagnoses or values that have no basis in the transcript, contextual notes or clinical note. Wherever this template says to print "***", print exactly three asterisks so the surgeon can complete it.)

(IMAGING READ: The surgeon may dictate an imaging read in clinical terms before or after the patient is in the room, or type it into the contextual notes. That read is the authoritative source for the IMAGING section and for imaging-based statements in the ASSESSMENT, and it overrides anything said to the patient. If there is more than one read, the later one wins.)
